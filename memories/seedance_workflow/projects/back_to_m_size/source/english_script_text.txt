The Day I Fit Into a Medium Aagin（英文版）
Chapter 1
Growing up, my childhood friend Luke and my friend Sophie had given me plenty of nicknames.
“Fatty.” “Piggy.” “Chubby.” “Butterball.”
They said those were the kinds of names only the people closest to you got to use.
Once, Sophie called me “Fatty” in front of my coworkers while I was giving a quarterly presentation.
The whole office burst out laughing. My Director frowned at me, and I finished the presentation with my face burning red.
Luke ruffled my hair.
“She didn’t mean anything by it. Don’t take it personally.”
Then, as usual, he added, “But you really should lose some weight. When clients see you looking like this…”
At a company retreat, Sophie imitated the way I stammered whenever I got nervous in front of clients.
She mimicked me constantly tugging down the hem of my blazer to hide my stomach. She even copied the squeak my chair made when I sat down.
Luke laughed louder than anyone.
“All right, stop. She’s sensitive.”
Then he turned around and saved the video of Sophie mocking me to his Instagram Highlights.
Last week, at a client appreciation dinner, I closed a million-dollar deal on my own for the first time.
Sophie raised her glass.
“Wow, our Fatty actually landed a deal? The client didn’t just feel sorry for you, did they?”
The client’s expression changed.
After dinner, they told me they wanted to reconsider the partnership.
The million-dollar deal was gone.
I sat in my car wiping away tears when Luke knocked on the window and climbed in. He handed me a tissue.
“Stop crying. Next time, just make sure she doesn’t drink so much.”
He paused.
“And don’t start eating every time you’re upset. The more you eat, the more—”
“The fatter I get. I know.”
I finished the sentence for him.
I dried my tears, opened my laptop, and submitted my resignation.
Eight years.
From high school until now, there had been a time when I wanted to keep walking beside them for much longer.
I tossed the tissue back into Luke’s lap, rolled up the window, and started the car.
Dear Fatty, congratulations.
You finally learned how to leave on your own.
Chapter 2
“Who approved your resignation?”
Luke’s voice came through the phone, calm and casual, as if he were asking what I wanted for lunch.
I wedged the phone between my shoulder and ear while continuing to stuff clothes into my suitcase.
“HR. I followed the normal process.”
“I told you not to do anything impulsive.”
He paused, then softened his voice.
“I’ll talk to the client again about the deal. I’ve got some connections with the manager.”
I zipped up my suitcase and glanced at the plane ticket on the table.
London.
One way.
“Luke, don’t bother.”
“What is this now?”
A trace of impatience finally entered his voice.
“You know Sophie talks without thinking. It’s not like this is news to you. Is it really worth all this?”
Was it worth it?
A million-dollar deal.
Three months of follow-ups.
Forty-seven emails.
Eleven revisions to the proposal.
“I’m not throwing a tantrum,” I said calmly.
I slipped the ticket into the side pocket of my backpack.
“I just want a change of scenery.”
“What change of scenery? You barely passed your English proficiency exam. Where are you even going?”
I didn’t answer.
He sighed again.
That familiar sigh, the one that said he was already used to putting up with me.
“Fine. Take a couple days to calm down. Sophie and I will take you out this weekend, and we’ll talk everything through.”
“I won’t be here this weekend.”
The other end went silent.
“What do you mean?”
“My flight’s the day after tomorrow.”
This time, the silence lasted longer.
I heard him rummaging around for something, probably cigarettes.
“Where are you going?”
“London. A friend’s company is hiring. I’m going to give it a shot.”
“You…”
He sounded almost speechless.
“You’re serious?”
I looked at myself in the mirror.
My eyes were still swollen. My lips were cracked, and my face was puffy from crying. I looked dull and exhausted.
“I’m serious.”
“Sarah.”
He suddenly said my full name, his voice dropping.
“Are you doing this to get back at me?”
I almost laughed.
Eight years, and he still thought every decision I made came from some emotional impulse.
“No.”
“Then tell me what’s really going on. Why are you suddenly leaving?”
Chapter 3
Why?
Because you and Sophie turned me into a joke together, and you didn’t even realize you were hurting me.
Because every time I worked hard for something, Sophie could erase it with one careless sentence, and you would always stand beside her and say, “She didn’t mean it.”
Because I was finally tired.
“I just want to see somewhere new.”
“By yourself? You can’t even take the subway without going the wrong—”
“I’m hanging up,” I cut him off. “I still have packing to do.”
“Sarah.”
I ended the call.
The second my screen went dark, a message from Sophie popped up.
“Sarah, I heard you’re quitting? Are you kidding me? LOL. What are you even going to do without me and Luke? You probably won’t even find clothes in your size by yourself in London. Stop being dramatic. Come out this weekend. Dinner’s on me, okay? My apology treat!”
I stared at the part about not finding clothes in my size for a long time.
I didn’t reply.
I tossed my phone onto the bed and wheeled my suitcase into the living room.
A hair clip Sophie had left behind the week before was still on the coffee table, a glittery little bow.
That day, she had worn a size small dress that hung loose around her waist while complaining that she’d “gotten so fat lately.”
I picked up the clip.
Then I dropped it into the trash.
The doorbell rang.
When I opened the door, Sophie was standing there.
She wore a cream knit dress, her hair styled in loose waves, with a bag of fruit in one hand.
“You’re actually packing?”
She peeked inside and widened her eyes dramatically.
“Oh my God, Sarah. You’re really leaving?”
I stepped aside and let her in.
She put the fruit on the coffee table, then turned around.
Her eyes were actually red.
“Is this because of what happened last time? I told you, I was drunk. Sarah, you’re not seriously leaving just because…”
Her voice faded.
She lowered her head and twisted the hem of her dress between her fingers.
“What am I supposed to do if you leave?”
Chapter 4
That was Sophie.
She always knew exactly when to look vulnerable.
She always knew how to make everyone think I was the one hurting her.
“This has nothing to do with you,” I said. “I want to leave.”
She looked up at me with watery eyes.
“Really? You’re not mad at me?”
“No.”
“Then… can you stay?”
She grabbed my hand.
“Or at least wait until Luke gets back? He’ll figure out a way to help you—”
“Sophie.”
I pulled my hand free.
“My flight leaves at six the morning after tomorrow.”
She froze.
Something flashed through those beautiful eyes.
It disappeared too quickly for me to tell what it was.
Then she smiled and reached up to smooth a stray lock of hair away from my forehead.
“Then I’ll come see you off.”
“You don’t have to—”
“I do.”
She looked at me seriously.
“You’re my best friend.”
My best friend.
As she said those words, I remembered her calling me Fatty in front of a client three months ago.
I remembered the video of her imitating me tugging at my clothes being shared more than two hundred times.
And I remembered Luke laughing harder than anyone.
“Whatever,” I said.
She happily wrapped her arms around me and rested her chin on my shoulder.
“Sarah, don’t forget me once you get there, okay?”
I said nothing.
As she let go, my phone buzzed.
A message from Luke.
“I don’t agree.”
I looked at it for two seconds.
Then I turned off my phone.
Chapter 5
After Sophie left, I kept packing.
At the very back of a drawer, I found a metal box filled with little things I had collected over the past eight years.
A birthday card Luke gave me during our sophomore year of high school.
“Happy birthday, Sarah. Hope you get braver every year.”
A friendship bracelet Sophie had made, red cord with a tiny bead.
The day she made it, she tried to slip it over my wrist.
It dug into my skin, leaving a red mark.
She gasped.
“Oops. Too tight. I’ll make you another one after you lose some weight.”
So it stayed in that box for eight years.
There were photos too.
Pictures of the three of us.
I was always on the edge, unconsciously turning my body sideways because I was afraid I took up too much space.
And somehow, I was always the one smiling the hardest.
I put the metal box on the highest shelf of my closet, somewhere I couldn’t easily reach.
I didn’t take it with me.
But I didn’t throw it away either.
At eleven that night, Luke called again.
I didn’t answer.
He sent six messages.
I didn’t read them.
The seventh was a voice message. I accidentally tapped it, and his voice filled the room.
“Sarah, you’ve hung up on me six times. Are you trying to start a fight? If you have something to say, say it. Don’t give me this silent-treatment crap.”
Silent treatment.
I gave a bitter laugh.
Me quietly packing my bags was the silent treatment.
So what did we call eight years of Luke and Sophie humiliating me in public?
Warm, loving support?
I turned off my phone.
Then I slept better than I had in a long time.
The next day was my last day before leaving.
I went to the office to finish the paperwork for my resignation.
My desk had already been cleared.
There was a sticky note on my monitor from the coworker who sat beside me.
“Good luck, Sarah.”
I peeled it off, tucked it into my pocket, and went to HR for the final signatures.
When I stepped out of the elevator, I ran into Luke.
He stood in the lobby in a perfectly tailored suit, one hand in his pocket.
The expression on his face was complicated.
“What are you doing here?”
“Picking you up.”
“I don’t need a ride.”
He stepped in front of me and looked down at me.
“Sarah, can you please talk to me like a normal person?”
“I am.”
He frowned and raised his hand to touch my head, just like he had for the past eight years.
Whenever he used to ruffle my hair, he would casually call me “our little piggy.”
Back then, I had actually thought it was affectionate.
I stepped back.
His hand froze in midair.
“You really are mad.”
He lowered it.
“I told you I’d talk to the manager again—”
“The deal’s gone. It’s gone.”
I cut him off.
“Does that really mean you have to quit?”
“Yes.”
He took a deep breath and switched to that gentle voice he always used on me.
“Sarah—”
“Don’t call me Sarah like that.”
He stopped.
In eight years, I had never interrupted him like this.
He was used to my easygoing temper.
Used to me putting up with everything.
Used to me nodding no matter what he said.
“Fine.”
He took a step back.
“Let’s go somewhere and sit down. We’ll talk.”
“There’s nothing to talk about.”
“You’re flying to London tomorrow. Alone. Who do you even know there? You don’t know your way around—”
“I will.”
There it was again.
That expression.
Like he was looking at a clueless child.
“Sophie’s been crying all day,” he suddenly said. “She thinks you’re leaving because of her.”
I was silent for a second.
“Oh. So?”
Luke’s expression changed.
“What do you mean, so? Now you won’t even accept her apology?”
I looked at him.
He was always defending Sophie.
I lost a deal, and apparently I should’ve stopped her from drinking.
She mocked me, and apparently I was too sensitive.
I resigned and left, and apparently I was overreacting.
But Sophie cried?
His first instinct was to come make me forgive her.
“Luke,” I asked calmly, “have you ever thought about something?”
“What?”
“If Sophie had been the one humiliated in front of that client, would you have said, ‘Just make sure Sarah doesn’t drink so much next time’?”
He froze.
His mouth opened.
Nothing came out.
I walked around him and pushed open the lobby’s glass doors.
The sunlight outside was painfully bright.
He followed me for two steps.
“Sarah, what time’s your flight? I’ll drive you.”
“No.”
“I’ll drive you.”
He repeated it like there was no room for argument.
I didn’t turn around.
“I don’t need you to anymore.”
The moment I walked out of the building, I let out a long breath.
For the first time in eight years, the road ahead felt so short that I couldn’t understand why it had taken me so long to walk it.
When I got home, there was a bouquet on my coffee table.
Sunflowers.
My favorite.
The card was in Sophie’s handwriting.
“Sarah, I’m seeing you off tomorrow. No refusing. Promise you’ll always be my best friend.”
I dropped the flowers into the trash beside the glittery bow clip.
Chapter 6
My alarm went off three times at four in the morning before I finally dragged myself out of bed.
By the time I washed up and wheeled my suitcase outside, the hallway was pitch-black.
The elevator doors opened.
Sophie was standing inside.
She wore a pink hoodie, her hair thrown into a messy bun. She had no makeup on, and there were dark circles under her eyes.
She grinned and grabbed my suitcase.
“Come on. Luke’s waiting downstairs.”
I looked at her.
“I told you I didn’t need anyone to see me off.”
“Oh, come on.”
She hooked her arm through mine and dragged me toward the exit.
“Who knows when you’ll be back? What’s wrong with letting us take you to the airport?”
Luke’s car sat beneath a streetlight downstairs, the engine still running.
He got out, took my suitcase, and put it in the trunk without saying much.
The car was quiet.
Sophie sat in the passenger seat.
I sat in the back.
Ten minutes into the drive, she suddenly turned around.
“Sarah, make sure you eat properly over there. Your stomach’s always been sensitive.”
“Mm-hmm.”
“And don’t reach for food whenever you’re stressed. You already have…”
She stopped herself.
“You already get nervous easily. Too much stress will make you lose sleep.”
“I know.”
“Video call me when you miss me. What’s the time difference again? Eight hours? When you’re asleep, I’ll probably just be getting off work—”
“Sophie,” Luke interrupted. “Let her have some quiet.”
Sophie stuck out her tongue and turned back around.
Luke’s eyes flicked toward me in the rearview mirror.
I looked away and watched the streetlights race past the window.
At the airport, Luke pushed my suitcase while Sophie clung to my arm.
The three of us walked together, just like always.
Except this time, I was in the middle instead of on the edge.
Because this would be the last time.
At the check-in counter, I finished the process and got my boarding pass.
Sophie hugged me tightly.
“Sarah, you idiot.”
She buried her face against my shoulder, her voice muffled.
“Who am I supposed to go shopping with after you leave?”
I patted her back.
“You have Luke.”
She let me go, wiped the corner of her eye, then glanced at Luke as if giving him a signal.
Luke stood a few feet away, holding something.
He walked over and handed me a brown paper bag.
“Food for the flight. And…”
He hesitated.
“Text me when you land.”
I took the bag and nodded.
Then I turned toward security.
“Sarah.”
Luke called after me.
I stopped but didn’t turn around.
“If things don’t work out over there, you can always come back.”
There was the faintest trace of uncertainty in his voice.
A little panic.
But only a little.
Because he believed I would come back.
He believed I couldn’t live without them.
For eight years, he had always believed that.
I said nothing.
I dragged my suitcase into the security line.
The line was long.
I looked back once.
From a distance, I saw Sophie talking animatedly to Luke, gesturing with both hands.
Luke was looking down at his phone, typing something.
Neither of them looked particularly sad.
They looked as relaxed as if they had just watched someone run out for groceries and expected her back any minute.
I turned around.
Gate B12.
I found a seat and opened the bag Luke had given me.
A sandwich.
A bottle of water.
Mints.
A bag of chips.
And beneath everything else, a sticky note.
Sophie’s round handwriting covered it.
“Fatty, the outside world is scary! You definitely won’t last long before you miss us. When that happens, Luke and I will come bring you home! It’s not like you can control your appetite anyway!”
I stared at the word Fatty for a long time.
As the plane taxied down the runway, I tore the note in half and stuffed it back into the bag.
Twelve hours later, I landed in London and turned on my phone.
Thirty-seven messages.
Most were memes and voice messages from Sophie.
Luke had sent only one.
“Tell me when you land.”
I replied with two words.
“I’m here.”
Then I closed the conversation.
My friend Emma was waiting at arrivals, holding a cardboard sign with an ugly little stick figure drawn on it.
“Sarah, welcome to your new life.”
I wheeled my suitcase toward her.
The London air was cold and damp.
I took a deep breath anyway.
No one here would call me Fatty.
Chapter 7
My first week in London, I was still fighting jet lag.
One night, I woke up at three and couldn’t fall asleep again.
I sat by the bay window of my rental, staring at the streetlights outside, when my phone lit up.
Sophie had posted on Instagram.
Nine photos.
Every single one was of her and Luke.
Restaurants. Movie theaters. Shopping centers.
The caption read:
“Life’s so boring without Sarah. Luke, you’re responsible for entertaining me now.”
Our old coworkers piled into the comments.
“You two gonna make it official already?”
“Childhood-friends romance is dead. Bestie swoops in?”
“Who’s gonna be our third wheel now that Sarah’s gone? LOL.”
Luke replied to one.
“Stop making things up.”
Sophie answered with a blushing emoji.
I stared at the screen for a long time.
I couldn’t quite name what I felt.
It wasn’t jealousy.
It was something colder.
The despair of having something I already knew finally confirmed.
After I left, their lives hadn’t developed a missing piece.
They looked more complete.
Without the heavy, unnecessary third person around, they could finally stand naturally beside each other.
Emma pushed open the door carrying two mugs of hot chocolate.
“What are you doing awake in the middle of the—”
She glanced at my screen.
“Is that the best friend you grew up with?”
“Yeah.”
Emma shoved a mug into my hands.
“Enough. Put the phone down. Tomorrow’s your first day at work.”
I took a sip.
The hot chocolate burned the tip of my tongue.
I muted Sophie on Instagram.
Then Luke.
My first day at work felt unfamiliar in every possible way.
New coworkers.
A completely different pace.
I struggled to follow meetings and took so many notes my hand hurt.
But no one called me Fatty.
No one mimicked the way I tugged at my clothes.
And when I finally did something right, no one said, “Wow, she actually managed it.”
At lunch, a coworker complimented one detail in my proposal.
It was an ordinary compliment.
I almost cried.
That weekend, I stepped onto the bathroom scale for the first time.
The numbers flickered.
154 pounds.
I stared at them, then raised my eyes in the mirror.
The woman looking back at me had rounded shoulders and almost no definition between her chin and neck.
“Want to come with me?”
Emma asked from the kitchen the next morning.
“I hit the gym at six. It’s right around the corner.”
I looked at her.
“Okay.”
By the second week, I could walk on the treadmill without gripping the rails.
By the third, I switched lunch to chicken breast and salad and stopped eating late-night snacks.
In the fourth week, a coworker looked at me in the break room.
“Sarah, have you lost weight?”
By week six, I bought a pair of medium jeans.
The last time I had fit into a medium, I was in middle school.
My hands shook as I pulled them on.
Not from excitement.
From unfamiliarity.
So this was what my own body could feel like when it was lighter.
By week eight, the scale read 120.
Thirty-four pounds.
I touched my face.
Then suddenly, I started crying.
I wasn’t sad.
For the first time, I felt like this body belonged to me.
I hadn’t started running to make them regret anything.
I wasn’t trying to stand in front of someone someday and make them look at me differently.
I only wanted to know what the real Sarah looked like once I peeled away everything that had been weighing me down.
Layer by layer.
Luke’s messages changed too.
One message a day became three.
Then one every morning, afternoon, and night.
At first, they sounded like normal concern.
Then they started getting strange.
“Who did you have lunch with today?”
“Was that coworker a guy or a girl?”
“You’re not working this weekend. Why aren’t you answering?”
I either replied with a few words or didn’t answer at all.
Then came the message that morning.
“I’ll be in London next week for work.”
A moment later, another appeared.
“There are some things I need to tell you in person.”
I stared at it.
I didn’t reply.
On Friday, I walked out of my office after working late.
Dusk had just settled over London.
Someone stood at the bottom of the steps.
He looked up when he saw me.
Then he froze.
His gaze dropped from my face to my shoulders, my waist, the jeans that now hung noticeably loose around me.
Then his eyes returned to my face.
His lips parted.
His Adam’s apple moved.
There was naked astonishment in his eyes.
“Sarah?!”

Chapter 8
I stayed on the steps.
Luke walked forward twice, then stopped as if he thought I might run.
“You…”
His voice was hoarse.
“How much weight did you lose?”
I didn’t answer.
He took a deep breath, as if rearranging everything in his head.
“I came this time,” he said, “because of Sophie.”
I looked at him.
“She confessed to me last month. She said she’s always liked me.”
The air seemed to freeze.
Whatever vague, indescribable thing had still been lingering inside me finally shattered.
He had flown all this way.
Not because he missed me.
He had come to tell me this in person and wait for me to graciously give them my blessing.
“Congratulations.”
I forced the corner of my mouth upward.
He seemed relieved.
“You’re not mad?”
I shook my head.
“Fatty—”
“Sarah,” I corrected him.
He froze again.
He had done a lot of that tonight.
“Right. Sarah. Sophie said she wanted to apologize. When you come back, maybe the three of us can still—”
“I’m not coming back.”
Chapter 9
His expression changed.
“What do you mean?”
“I mean I’m not coming back, Luke.”
London was completely dark now.
Streetlights flickered one by one.
I looked at the face I had known better than any other for eight years.
Then I smiled.
“Goodbye.”
I walked past him toward the Underground.
No footsteps followed me.
His reflection appeared in the glass doors behind me.
He stood exactly where I had left him.
He didn’t follow.
Maybe he was still trying to process what he had just seen.
The round Sarah he had known forever, the girl who had always followed behind him, had become someone he could no longer simply expect to keep up.
I didn’t sleep at all that night.
The next morning, I showed up at work with dark circles under my eyes.
Emma took one look at me and silently refilled my espresso.
“Those two again?”
“Last time.”
She snorted but didn’t ask anything else.
The workweek moved fast.
My new project was so intense I barely had time to think about anything else.
I handled the international e-commerce side and had to negotiate directly with clients in English.
At first, I stumbled.
Then I got better.
During our weekly meeting, my Director singled out my proposal for praise, saying the perspective was original and the data was solid.
No one added, “Wow, she actually did it.”
It was just praise.
Normal recognition.
When I stood to thank him, my voice was steady.
It didn’t shake once.
That evening, I received an unexpected message.
Not from Luke.
Not Sophie.
It was from Nathan.
He was my class president in high school and sat behind me in class.
“Sarah? Heard you’re in London. I’ll be there for work next week. Want to grab dinner?”
Nathan had later gone to Cambridge for graduate school and stayed in London to work in finance.
We had never been close, but we weren’t strangers either.
It had been a simple classmate relationship.
No nicknames.
No mocking.
Just the occasional “Thanks” when one of us borrowed the other’s notes.
“Sure,” I replied.
Friday night, he took me to a restaurant by the Thames.
He wore a sharp suit and seemed much taller than I remembered, but his smile was exactly the same.
“You’ve changed a lot.”
He pushed the menu toward me.
“You seem more confident. And… you’ve lost a lot of weight. I almost didn’t recognize you.”
“Really?”
“Back in high school, you barely looked people in the eye when you talked. You always ordered the largest uniform size and hid in the back row.”
He smiled.
“You’re different now.”
I looked down at the menu, my ears were warming.
“Maybe it’s the environment.”
“What kind of environment?”
“One where nobody calls me Fatty.”
His hand stopped.
“Who called you Fatty?”
“No one anymore.”
I smiled.
“It’s in the past.”
Dinner was easy.
He talked about work, life in London, and things that had happened back in high school.
Not once did he say anything that made me uncomfortable.
When the check came, he said, “If you ever need anything here, call me. You don’t have to handle everything alone.”
“Okay.”
On the way home, I posted my first Instagram update since moving to London.
A photo of the Thames at night.
“Dinner with a new friend. London looks beautiful tonight.”
Five minutes later, Sophie commented.
“Wow, Sarah has new friends now! Is it a guy? Don’t tell me you’re getting scammed over there. You’ve always been terrible at reading people… Lost a little weight and suddenly you think you’re all that?”
I stared at it for two seconds.
Then deleted her comment.
Three minutes later, Luke called.
I didn’t answer.
He texted instead.
“Who did you have dinner with?”
“A classmate.”
“Guy or girl?”
“What does that have to do with you?”
Chapter 10
He went silent for a long time.
Then he said something I hadn’t expected.
“Sarah, Sophie and I aren’t together.”
I froze.
“Didn’t you say she confessed to you?”
“She did. I didn’t say yes.”
“But last time—”
“You asked whether I’d accepted, and I didn’t answer.”
His voice sounded rough.
“You turned around and left.”
I leaned against the back seat of the cab and watched the city lights streak past outside.
How ridiculous.
When he stayed silent, it meant no.
When I stayed silent, everyone treated it as yes.
Because he never explained himself.
He simply assumed I should understand.
“What’s the point of telling me now?” I asked.
He didn’t answer immediately.
Several seconds passed.
“Sarah, there are things I didn’t understand before.”
“And you understand them now?”
“I’m trying to.”
“Then take your time.”
I hung up.
The cab stopped outside my apartment.
It was eleven at night in London, and the wind was cold.
Yet I felt strangely calm.
It wasn’t that I no longer cared.
I just didn’t need his answer anymore.
When I went upstairs, Emma was watching a show in the living room.
“Well, look who’s back from her date.”
“It wasn’t a date.”
“Sure, sure.”
She didn’t even look up.
“Your phone’s vibrated about eight hundred times.”
I checked it.
Sophie had sent a long voice message.
I played one sentence.
“Sarah, did you mute me? What was that supposed to mean, deleting my comment? I only said it because I care about—”
I stopped the message.
Marked it as read.
And didn’t reply.
That night, I slept perfectly.
I didn’t dream once.
Chapter 11
By my third month in London, my project won the best project of the quarter.
When my Director announced it in front of the whole team, everyone applauded.
Someone whistled.
I stood there with my ears ringing.
Not from nerves.
From the strange disbelief of finally being recognized and still not quite knowing how to accept it.
Emma gave me a thumbs-up from her desk.
“Sarah, you’re buying dinner.”
So that night, I really did take a few people from the team out.
Everyone talked and laughed.
No one mocked my accent or my expressions.
No one suggested, “Let’s order Sarah dessert. You know she’ll want it.”
I held a glass of water and listened to everyone's toast.
On the way home, I saw a post online.
An old coworker had uploaded screenshots from our former company group chat.
Sophie’s messages.
“How long do you think Sarah will last in London? I give her three months before she comes crying home.”
“She can barely run on a treadmill. And she thinks she’s gonna lose weight? LOL.”
“She stress-eats whenever she’s alone. She’ll probably come back even fatter.”
“She’s terrified of being alone. First night of freshman orientation, she literally hugged her pillow and cried because she thought her roommate didn’t like her.”
Several people laughed.
Someone agreed vaguely.
Others just responded with ellipses.

“So I wanted to tell you I’m sorry.”
The night wind swept in from the river.
In eight years, this was the first time Luke had ever apologized to me.
Not, “Don’t be mad.”
Not, “She didn’t mean it.”
Not, “Just be more careful next time.”
I’m sorry.
“Luke.”
“Yeah?”
“That apology came way too late.”
His voice fell.
“I know.”
“And you know what else?”
“What?”
I looked into his eyes.
“I don’t need it anymore.”
The streetlight cast shadows across his face.
I saw his fingers curl into a fist, then slowly loosen.
“Sarah, I—”
“Don’t.”
I cut him off, my voice calmed.
“Go home.”
I walked around him and entered the apartment building.
As the door closed, his reflection appeared in the glass.
He was still standing there.
He didn’t follow me.
The elevator doors slid shut.
I leaned against the wall, closed my eyes for a moment, and took a deep breath.
Emma opened the apartment door and took one look at me.
“What happened?”
“Nothing.”
I smiled, kicked off my shoes, and walked inside.
“Set an alarm for me before tomorrow’s morning meeting, okay?”
“Working late again?”
“No.”
I dropped onto the couch and stared at the ceiling.
“I just want to get to the office early tomorrow.”
Because tomorrow was a new day.
New work.
A new life that belonged entirely to me.
I didn’t need anyone’s apology before I could move forward.
I didn’t need anyone to chase me to prove I was worth wanting.
Sarah was Sarah.
Not Fatty.
Not Piggy.
Not Butterball.
Not anyone’s accessory.
And I wasn’t someone who needed a number on a scale to prove her worth.
Still, I was glad about those thirty-four pounds.
They were proof of how hard I had fought to take myself back from them.
Outside, the lights of London burned all night.
I slept deeply.
And I didn’t dream.
